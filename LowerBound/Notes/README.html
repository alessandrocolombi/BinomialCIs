Model and target

We observe independent counts Nj∼Bin⁡(n,pj),j≥1, N_j\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p_j),\qquad j\ge1, with fixed prevalences satisfying S(p)=∑j≥1pj<∞. S(p)=\sum_{j\ge1}p_j<\infty. Define Mmax=maxj:Nj=0pj,Mr=∑j:Nj=0pjr,ℳ0=M1. M_{\max}=\max_{j:N_j=0}p_j,\qquad M_r=\sum_{j:N_j=0}p_j^r,\qquad \mathcal M_0=M_1. Use zero for the maximum of an empty set. For r at least one, summability ensures that the moments are finite. Finite-support prevalence sequences, extended by zero prevalences, are included in this model.

For n at least four, the construction below yields a nonnegative lower endpoint satisfying infp:S(p)<∞Prp{Ln≤Mmax}≥1−α. \inf_{p:S(p)<\infty}\Pr_p\{L_n\le M_{\max}\}\ge1-\alpha. This is a fixed-sample-size confidence guarantee, not a simultaneous-in-time or optional-stopping guarantee.

Why a missing-mass lower bound alone does not suffice

The deterministic inequality is Mmax≤M1M_{\max}\le M_1. A fixed unseen mass s can be spread over k unseen categories of prevalence s/k, making the maximum arbitrarily small as k grows. Thus a lower bound for M1 alone cannot imply a strictly positive numerical lower bound for Mmax under summability alone.

For r>s≥1r>s\ge1, however, Mr≤Mmax⁡r−sMs,Mmax≥(MrMs)1/(r−s) M_r\le M_{\max}^{r-s}M_s, \qquad M_{\max}\ge\left(\frac{M_r}{M_s}\right)^{1/(r-s)} when Ms is positive. In particular, Mmax≥M2/M1M_{\max}\ge M_2/M_1. If M1 is zero, Mmax is zero as well, and the lower endpoint is set to zero.

Write Kn,k=∑j𝟏{Nj=k},Kn=∑j𝟏{Nj>0},Mr=∑j:Nj=0pjr,M1=ℳ0. K_{n,k}=\sum_j\mathbf 1\{N_j=k\},\qquad K_n=\sum_j\mathbf 1\{N_j>0\},\qquad M_r=\sum_{j:N_j=0}p_j^r,\qquad M_1=\mathcal M_0. Deterministic oracle lower endpoint

An oracle deterministic lower endpoint is the largest nonrandom lower endpoint satisfying the required coverage when the prevalence vector is known. For a finite-support prevalence vector, independence gives

Prp(Mmax<t)=∏j:pj≥t{1−(1−pj)n},t>0. \Pr_p(M_{\max}<t)=\prod_{j:p_j\ge t}\{1-(1-p_j)^n\},\qquad t>0.

The oracle is the largest point in {0}∪{pj:pj>0}\{0\}\cup\{p_j:p_j>0\} such that this strict lower tail is at most α\alpha. It is thus the largest deterministic endpoint with Pr⁡p(Mmax≥oracle)≥1−α\Pr_p(M_{\max}\ge\mathrm{oracle})\ge1-\alpha, with exact handling of equality at probability atoms. This oracle is not the realized MmaxM_{\max} for each sample, which would be exactly known if both p and that sample were supplied, nor is it the moment ratio M2/M1M_2/M_1. An adaptive lower-bound median can exceed this deterministic oracle without contradiction; coverage is a statement about its joint behavior with Mmax.

The oracle lower endpoint can genuinely be zero. In particular, if Pr⁡p(Mmax=0)>α\Pr_p(M_{\max}=0)>\alpha, every strictly positive deterministic endpoint fails the required lower-coverage guarantee. This does not require Mmax=0M_{\max}=0 in every repetition.

Interpretation and loss from the moment ratio

The ratio Rn=M2/M1R_n=M_2/M_1 is a prevalence-weighted mean of the unseen prevalences, rather than their maximum. It equals Mmax when all positive unseen prevalences are equal, but can be substantially smaller otherwise. When M2 is positive, LnMmax=L2M2M1U1M2M1Mmax. \frac{L_n}{M_{\max}}= \frac{L_2}{M_2}\frac{M_1}{U_1}\frac{M_2}{M_1M_{\max}}. This separates the statistical losses in the two confidence bounds from the intrinsic loss of using the second-to-first moment ratio.

When M1>0M_1>0, the deterministic lower bounds Br=(MrM1)1/(r−1),r>1, B_r=\left(\frac{M_r}{M_1}\right)^{1/(r-1)},\qquad r>1, are nondecreasing in r and converge to Mmax. Indeed, with wj=pj/M1w_j=p_j/M_1 on the unseen categories, they are weighted power means: Br=(∑j:Nj=0wjpjr−1)1/(r−1),∑jwj=1. B_r=\left(\sum_{j:N_j=0}w_jp_j^{r-1}\right)^{1/(r-1)}, \qquad\sum_jw_j=1. Optimizing estimated lower bounds over r requires simultaneous confidence control. Monotonicity of the true moments does not ensure that estimated endpoints improve with r.

Relation to the lower bounds in the submitted manuscript

Theorems 3 and 6 in Binomial_CIs_jasa_sub lower-bound the attainable size of any uniformly valid upper confidence bound Tn along least-favourable sequences of prevalence vectors. They are minimax limitations on procedures, not lower confidence bounds for Mmax for every prevalence vector.

The new construction instead bounds Mmax from below, for every fixed summable prevalence sequence and at finite n. It complements an upper confidence bound, rather than strengthening the minimax theorem. If an upper endpoint Tn has error delta/2 and the lower endpoint has error delta/2, the union bound gives Prp{Ln(δ/2)≤Mmax≤Tn(δ/2)}≥1−δ. \Pr_p\{L_n(\delta/2)\le M_{\max}\le T_n(\delta/2)\}\ge1-\delta. A lower bound on the upper endpoint Tn is not automatically a lower bound on the length Tn minus Ln of this two-sided interval. No optimality claim for the new lower endpoint or interval length has been established.

Two-sided confidence bounds for an unseen moment of fixed order

Fix an integer r≥2r\ge2, n≥2rn\ge2r, and δ∈(0,1)\delta\in(0,1). The following construction is valid for every fixed summable prevalence sequence; no regular variation or knowledge of S(p) is needed. It is an explicit conservative interval, not an optimality claim.

Set ar=(nr)−1,Tr=arKn,r,Dr=Tr−Mr,ηk=𝔼pKn,k. a_r=\binom nr^{-1},\qquad T_r=a_rK_{n,r},\qquad D_r=T_r-M_r,\qquad \eta_k=\mathbb E_pK_{n,k}. Independence across categories implies 0≤𝔼pDr≤br:=rηr+1(nr+1),Var⁡p(Dr)≤vr:=ar2ηr+η2r(n2r). 0\le\mathbb E_pD_r\le b_r:=\frac{r\eta_{r+1}}{\binom n{r+1}},\qquad \operatorname{Var}_p(D_r)\le v_r:=a_r^2\eta_r+ \frac{\eta_{2r}}{\binom n{2r}}.

Indeed, writing qj=1−pjq_j=1-p_j, the coordinate mean is 𝔼pdr,j=pjrqjn−r(1−qjr)≤rpjr+1qjn−r−1,dr,j=ar𝟏{Nj=r}−pjr𝟏{Nj=0}. \mathbb E_pd_{r,j}=p_j^rq_j^{n-r}(1-q_j^r) \le r p_j^{r+1}q_j^{n-r-1},\qquad d_{r,j}=a_r\mathbf1\{N_j=r\}-p_j^r\mathbf1\{N_j=0\}. For the variance, use disjointness of the two indicator events: Var⁡(Dr)≤∑j𝔼[dr,j2]=ar2ηr+∑jpj2rqjn≤ar2ηr+η2r(n2r). \operatorname{Var}(D_r)\le\sum_j\mathbb E[d_{r,j}^2] =a_r^2\eta_r+\sum_jp_j^{2r}q_j^n \le a_r^2\eta_r+\frac{\eta_{2r}}{\binom n{2r}}. Since dr,j≤ard_{r,j}\le a_r and its mean is nonnegative, each centered coordinate is at most ara_r. One-sided Bernstein yields Prp{Dr>br+2vrx+2arx/3}≤e−x. \Pr_p\{D_r>b_r+\sqrt{2v_rx}+2a_rx/3\}\le e^{-x}. For the other tail, nonnegativity of the mean and Cantelli’s inequality give Prp{Dr<−1−ϵϵvr}≤ϵ. \Pr_p\left\{D_r<-\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}v_r}\right\} \le\epsilon. If the variance is zero, this conclusion follows directly since Dr is a nonnegative constant. Summability ensures convergence of the countable sums and finite variance, so the inequalities apply to the infinite alphabet.

To eliminate the unknown expected counts, let ϵ=δ/5,x=log⁡(5/δ),Ck=(x/2+Kn,k+7x/6)2,k∈{r,r+1,2r}. \epsilon=\delta/5,\qquad x=\log(5/\delta),\qquad C_k=\left(\sqrt{x/2}+\sqrt{K_{n,k}+7x/6}\right)^2, \quad k\in\{r,r+1,2r\}. Each count bound ηk≤Ck\eta_k\le C_k has error at most epsilon, as in Ayed et al. (2019), Remark A.1. Define b̂r=rCr+1(nr+1),v̂r=ar2Cr+C2r(n2r). \widehat b_r=\frac{rC_{r+1}}{\binom n{r+1}},\qquad \widehat v_r=a_r^2C_r+\frac{C_{2r}}{\binom n{2r}}. Fully observable endpoints are Lr=[Tr−b̂r−2v̂rx−2arx3]+,Ur=Tr+1−ϵϵv̂r. \boxed{L_r=\left[T_r-\widehat b_r-\sqrt{2\widehat v_rx} -\frac{2a_rx}{3}\right]_+},\qquad \boxed{U_r=T_r+\sqrt{\frac{1-\epsilon}{\epsilon}\widehat v_r}}. The three count events, the Bernstein event and the Cantelli event have total failure probability at most 5ϵ=δ5\epsilon=\delta. On their intersection, Lr≤Mr≤Ur. \boxed{L_r\le M_r\le U_r}. Hence inf⁡p:S(p)<∞Pr⁡p(Lr≤Mr≤Ur)≥1−δ\inf_{p:S(p)<\infty}\Pr_p(L_r\le M_r\le U_r)\ge1-\delta. The argument does not require independence among those five events. The upper radius has a square-root inverse-error dependence because it uses Cantelli; it is not asserted to be the sharpest upper bound available.

If n is smaller than 2r, a conservative fallback valid for any positive n is [0,S¯][0,\overline S], since Mr≤S(p)M_r\le S(p) for r at least one. With Ŝ=n−1∑jNj\widehat S=n^{-1}\sum_jN_j and z=log⁡(1/δ)z=\log(1/\delta), the left-tail concentration of this sum gives S¯=(Ŝ+z/(2n)+z/(2n))2,Prp{S(p)≤S¯}≥1−δ. \overline S=\left(\sqrt{\widehat S+z/(2n)}+\sqrt{z/(2n)}\right)^2, \qquad\Pr_p\{S(p)\le\overline S\}\ge1-\delta.

For noninteger r≥2r\ge2, let k=⌊r⌋k=\lfloor r\rfloor. Since Mk+1≤Mr≤MkM_{k+1}\le M_r\le M_k, construct the above intervals for k and k+1, each with error delta/2. Then [Lk+1,Uk][L_{k+1},U_k] covers Mr with probability at least one minus delta, provided n≥2(k+1)n\ge2(k+1).

Non-strict inequalities are necessary for a nonnegative lower endpoint when Mr can be zero. Strict inequalities can be obtained by replacing Lr by Lr minus any positive constant and Ur by Ur plus a positive constant, at the cost of allowing a negative lower endpoint.

Monte Carlo coverage and oracle comparison

Empirical coverage of the random lower endpoint is 1Brep∑b=1Brep𝟏{Ln,b≤Mmax⁡,b}. \frac1{B_{\mathrm{rep}}}\sum_{b=1}^{B_{\mathrm{rep}}} \mathbf 1\{L_{n,b}\le M_{\max,b}\}. Both quantities use the same Monte Carlo sample. This differs from the fraction of lower endpoints below a deterministic oracle, and neither comparison can be replaced by comparing their medians. High coverage alone does not imply a tight lower endpoint: an identically zero endpoint has coverage one.
